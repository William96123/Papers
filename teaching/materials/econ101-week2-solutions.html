ECON 101, Fall 2020

Q1

Marginal benefit is the change in total enjoyment from one more flagon.

Number of Flagons Total Enjoyment Marginal Benefit
1 20 20
2 36 16
3 48 12
4 56 8
5 60 4
6 60 0
7 56 \(-4\)

Dontos keeps drinking as long as the next flagon is worth at least its price. At 4 copper stars he drinks 5 flagons (the fifth is worth exactly its price, so he is indifferent about it). At 0.5 copper stars he still drinks 5, because the sixth flagon adds nothing to his enjoyment and is not worth even a groat.

Q2

Marginal benefit falls by 4 with each flagon, so \(MB(Q) = 24 - 4Q\). He drinks until marginal benefit equals the price, so \(P = 24 - 4Q\), and his demand curve is \[Q_d = 6 - \frac{P}{4}, \qquad 0 \le P \le 24.\]

Q3

Marginal cost per flagon is the change in total cost over each block of 10 flagons, divided by 10.

Number of Flagons Total Cost Marginal Cost per flagon
10 1 0.1
20 3 0.2
30 6 0.3
40 10 0.4
50 15 0.5
60 21 0.6
70 28 0.7

The keeper sells as long as the price covers the marginal cost of the next flagon. At 0.4 copper stars he sells 40 flagons, since the marginal cost of each flagon up to the fortieth is at most 0.4 and the next one costs 0.5.

Q4

Marginal cost rises by 0.1 with every 10 flagons, so \(MC(Q) = Q/100\). Setting price equal to marginal cost gives the supply curve \[Q_s = 100P.\]

Q5

Market curves add individual quantities at each price. \[Q_D = 1000\left(6 - \frac{P}{4}\right) = 6000 - 250P, \qquad Q_S = 100 \times 100P = 10000P.\] Setting \(Q_D = Q_S\) gives \(10250P = 6000\), so \(P \approx 0.59\) copper stars and \(Q \approx 5854\) flagons, about 5.9 per drinker and 58.5 per tavern.

Q6

A fall in demand shifts the demand curve left, so both price and quantity fall. A rise in the cost of making beer shifts the supply curve left, so price rises and quantity falls. Price rises with quantity unchanged when demand rises and supply falls at the same time by offsetting amounts, for example a festival that raises demand during the wheat shortage. It also happens if demand is perfectly inelastic and supply falls.
