Tutorial 1 Notes (2026–27)

William Duan

September 8, 2026

These notes document what was covered in the tutorial session, for the benefit of future TAs. They are not intended as study material for students.

Topics

Course logistics (office hour schedule, etc.). Basics of preference and choice: definition of a rational preference relation (completeness and transitivity), the choice rule induced by a preference relation, the revealed preference relation, and the Weak Axiom of Revealed Preference (WARP). Main result: the induced choice rule satisfies WARP if and only if the underlying preference relation is rational. We replicated the full proof in class.

Objective

Set up logistics. Introduce the preference/choice framework and, through the WARP theorem, give students a first complete proof at the level of rigor expected in this course.

Comments

This is a change from last year, where the first session was spent reviewing basic real analysis (proof techniques, open/closed sets, continuity). This year that review was dropped and the first session went straight into preference and choice. The reason for the change: I personally ran the math camp for the incoming cohort this year, and did not want to simply repeat the math camp material. Future TAs should use their best judgement on whether it is necessary to redo some basic real analysis in the first session.

Sketch of the proof, for reference:

  • (\(\Leftarrow\)) If \(\succsim\) is rational, the induced choice rule \(C^*(B,\succsim) = \{x \in B : x \succsim y \ \forall y \in B\}\) satisfies WARP. Suppose \(x,y \in B\) with \(x \in C^*(B)\), and \(x,y \in B'\) with \(y \in C^*(B')\). Then \(x \succsim y\) (from \(B\)) and \(y \succsim z\) for all \(z \in B'\); transitivity gives \(x \succsim z\) for all \(z \in B'\), so \(x \in C^*(B')\).

  • (\(\Rightarrow\)) Conversely, if the choice rule satisfies WARP (with a sufficiently rich domain — budgets of two and three elements suffice), the revealed preference relation is complete and transitive: completeness from two-element budgets, transitivity from three-element budgets plus WARP.

Beyond the proof itself, we discussed at some length why this seemingly trivial result matters:

  1. It gives a single empirical test (WARP, stated entirely in terms of observable choices) that definitively determines whether the preference relation — an abstract and fundamentally unobservable object — is rational.

  2. It is one of those rare results where a property of observed behaviour can be traced back to a unique origin: WARP in choices corresponds exactly to rationality of the underlying preference, nothing more and nothing less.

A recurring issue from last year worth keeping in mind: students often use the conclusion to prove the conclusion. Walking through this proof carefully is a good opportunity to establish the distinction between premise and conclusion early.
